Upon learning, coding, and analyzing the three methods of pipelining, we could see how they differed, primarily in how they balance human judgment against automation. The manual pipeline was human driven from end to end where I grouped reviews into five coherent clusters, organized them into personas, and derived requirements and tests with a focus on clarity and concreteness. The automated pipeline relied on using a specific LLM model which was the Groq API with meta-llama/llaa-4-scout-17b-16e-instruct, which when I used it to provide the necessary pipeline, had demonstrated increased breadth and speed but also introduced vagueness and overgeneralization. The hybrid pipeline started from the automated drafts and applied human edits.
The clearest personas came from the manual pipeline which aligned clearly with the five review groups, providing reduced confusion and overlap. The manual pipeline’s ambiguity ratio was 0.0, whereas the automated and hybrid pipelines both showed an ambiguity ratio of 0.0238, reflecting the presence of vague or generic descriptors in AI-generated personas.
For requirements usefulness defined as specificity, implementability, and readiness,  manual pipeline again performed best where it produced 20 concrete requirements supported by 24 tests, with an ambiguity ratio of 0.0. The automated pipeline generated 28 requirements but only 6 tests, even with a higher testability rate of 0.2143 The hybrid pipeline also produced 28 requirements with 4 tests (testability rate 0.1429). In practice, the manual set was more implementable, while automated and hybrid increased variety and coverage at the cost of precision.
Traceability completeness was strong across all pipelines, each achieving a traceability ratio of 1.0, meaning every requirement was linked appropriately. Review coverage followed a similar pattern 0.0195 for automated and hybrid versus 0.0111 for manual indicating broader but still small coverage relative to the 4,515 cleaned reviews.
Overall, my expectations have stayed the same during the duration of this project suggesting that when a human creates manual reviews, personas, and code, it will triumph on clarity and usefulness where manually created personas and requirements are more coherent and precise. In contrast, automated pipelining has higher coverage in a speedy broad approach, but the personas and requirements provided are too vague and ambiguous. Hybrid objectively would be the best approach to these problems as it can provide concise information on the manual side and use automation to provide more coverage over the reviews.
